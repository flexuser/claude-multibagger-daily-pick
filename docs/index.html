Sedemac Mechatronics is a technology-focused auto component manufacturer specializing in electronic control units and controllers for two-wheelers and off-road vehicles. While the company demonstrates strong growth and high ROCE, it faces scrutiny regarding tax litigation and rising working capital intensity.

Management

The company is led by promoter Manish Sharma and a professional management team. It is backed by private equity firm Xponentia Capital, which has been trimming its stake post-listing. Management has maintained a clean record regarding share pledges, with formal disclosures confirming no encumbrances.

Additionally, it received a minor GST demand of Rs 40,036 in September 2026, though a much larger initial notice was dropped by authorities. No major auditor resignations or governance failures have been reported.

Latest business update

In Q4 FY26, the company reported significant capex of Rs 227 crore to support capacity expansion. Management emphasizes that as an auto-component supplier, they operate on a rolling supply basis rather than a traditional long-term order book model.

What would prove this wrong

Write these down before you buy. Check them each quarter.

  • Operating margin drops below 12% (now 16%).
  • ROCE falls below 22% (now 31%).
  • Promoter pledge appears or rises above 10%, or promoter holding falls more than 2 pp.
  • Operating cash flow stays below half of profit for two years running.
  • Weekly close below the 200-day average on rising volume.
  • Order book or order inflow stops growing faster than revenue at the next concall.

Verify before you act

Automated checks cannot settle these. They are often where a promising number turns out to be a trap.

  • Order book and its growth vs revenue (latest investor presentation or concall). Filing summaries are short; open the linked PDFs
  • Export share and export margins (annual report segment note). Only export order announcements are detected, not margins
  • Capex plan, how it is funded, and expected capacity timeline
  • FII and DII trend from the latest shareholding pattern, if it shows n/a above
  • Customer concentration and dependence on government orders
  • Governance: auditor changes, related-party dealings, promoter track record
  • Whether the growth is organic or came from a one-off order or acquisition
