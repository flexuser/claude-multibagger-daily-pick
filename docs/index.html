Sedemac Mechatronics is a technology-focused auto component manufacturer specializing in electronic control units (ECUs) and motor control units (MCUs) for two-wheelers. While the company demonstrates strong growth and high ROCE, the recent surge in receivable days and negative free cash flow, combined with ongoing tax litigation, warrants caution.

Management

The company was founded by alumni from IIT Bombay, including Shashikanth Suryanarayanan. Management has transitioned from a private entity to a listed company, but the recent block deal by early investors (Xponentia Group) suggests a potential exit strategy that retail investors should monitor closely.

Controversies and litigation

The company is currently contesting a Rs 74.93 lakh penalty from the Income Tax Department (FY 2017-18) regarding R&D deduction disallowances. Additionally, it recently settled GST demand notices in Tamil Nadu for FY22-24, where most allegations were dropped, leaving a minor demand of Rs 40,036.

Latest business update

Q1 FY27 performance was driven by the adoption of SLC-based ISG ECUs in high-volume motorcycles and increased E2W MCU volumes. The company reported a capex of Rs 227 crore in Q4 FY26 to support capacity expansion, though it does not maintain a traditional order book due to the nature of its supply contracts.

What would prove this wrong

Write these down before you buy. Check them each quarter.

  • Promoter pledge rises above 20% or promoter holding falls >3 pp in a year
  • Two consecutive quarters of revenue decline or margin compression >200 bps
  • Debt/equity crosses 1.5x without a clear, funded capacity plan
  • Credible SEBI / governance / auditor red flag in exchange filings
  • Thesis invalidated: structural theme demand collapses or customer concentration risk materialises

Verify before you act

Automated checks cannot settle these. They are often where a promising number turns out to be a trap.

  • Order book and its growth vs revenue (latest investor presentation or concall). Filing summaries are short; open the linked PDFs
  • Export share and export margins (annual report segment note). Only export order announcements are detected, not margins
  • Capex plan, how it is funded, and expected capacity timeline
  • FII and DII trend from the latest shareholding pattern, if it shows n/a above
  • Customer concentration and dependence on government orders
  • Governance: auditor changes, related-party dealings, promoter track record
  • Whether the growth is organic or came from a one-off order or acquisition
  • Government policy / PLI / tariff exposure: does demand depend on a scheme that can change?
  • Management commentary and guidance from the latest concall — not just the press release
  • Sector cycle: is the whole theme already crowded in price (theme relative strength on the page)
